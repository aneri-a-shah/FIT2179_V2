Exploring the International Hotspots

Popular for Australians

Australian outbound travel seems heavily dictated by geographic convenience (proximity and shorter flight times). We can see that that Indonesia and New Zealand look to be amongst the most visited countries in 2025, highlighting that shorter transit times are the primary drivers for Australian travelers.

While the Asia-Pacific dominates with the highest overall volume of Australian tourists, the consistent lines to Europe and the US indicate that despite prioritising closer locations, Australians still heavily invest in the more traditional "trip of a lifetime" long-haul locations.

The Aussie Dollar vs the World

There seems to be great elasticity of tourism demand, as destinations sitting in the top-right quadrant (notably Japan) prove that a highly favourable exchange rate acts as a major influencer for tourism growth.

On the other hand, the bottom-left quadrant depicts a story of economic friction. The strength of the US Dollar against the AUD has directly impacted travel volume, showing that even legacy/well-travelled holiday destinations are subject to currency pressure.

COVID-19 Tourism Effects and Recovery

Post-COVID travel didn't just bounce back to normal, it fundamentally shifted. It seems that the time in lockdown across 2020-2021 created a shift in Australian travelers, where they started prioritising travelling around post-lockdown. The massive surge of Australian tourism in many key countries speaks volumes, with the 233% increase for Japan and 199% increase for Vietnam showing a redirection of Australian tourist capital towards East and South-East Asia rather than a uniform global recovery.

Destinations in the red, such as the US, indicate that many barriers, whether that be inflation and exchange rates, flight fluctuations, shifting cultural interests or changing political climates, are preventing a return to the 2019 baseline.

Domestic Travel: Where Aussies Holiday at Home

Domestic tourism is overwhelmingly anchored around the east coastline of Australia, spanning across Queensland, New South Wales and Victoria. This trend seems to shed light on a reliance on established coastal infrastructure and major population hubs (e.g. capital cities).

In comparison, the emptiness of inner, central Australia, Northern Territory and Western Australia emphasise that domestic travel for most Australians means coastal travel, with the outback or more rural areas remaining a niche in tourism.

Battle of the States

The main three locations, New South Wales, Queensland and Victoria dominate the national tourism narrative here.

Despite significantly lower amounts of international visitors in the other States and Territories, every location managed to make an almost-full recovery back to pre-COVID numbers.

Day Trips vs Overnighters: Aussies on a Budget

Overnight trips tend to remain incredibly stable year-over-year, acting as a reliable baseline of the domestic tourism economy. Meanwhile, day trips showed a more volatile trend, acting as an economic buffer - when cost-of-living pressures hit during 2025, Australian travelers seemed to substitute expensive multi-day holidays for cheaper, high-volume day trips.

The states with the widest cost gaps between day trips and overnight stays (Northern Territory, Western Australia, Tasmania) are geographically isolated and receive less tourism overall compared to the other states. This could imply heavy logistical tax, as getting there requires special flights or longer transits, forcing the average overnight spend to skyrocket compared to the cheaper and more competitive overnight markets in Victoria and New South Wales.

Breaking Down the Budget

Transport and Food/Drinks are the undisputed anchors of the travel wallet, taking up over half of the budget year-round. Because Transport, Food/Drinks and Accommodation take up such a large collective chunk of the budget, not a lot remains for the Activities and Other category. This means that for activities like tours and other experiences, tour operators and experience providers are competing for the smallest, yet most highly contested fraction of the traveler's budget.
